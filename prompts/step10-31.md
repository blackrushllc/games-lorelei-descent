# JUNIE TASK — LORELEI: DESCENT
## Step 10.3.1 — Lorelei Automatic Spawn and Telekinetic Power Tuning

### Objective

Step 10.3 successfully introduced Lorelei's companion AI, and the developer has confirmed that her following behavior, movement speed, obstacle navigation, and animation timing work extremely well in actual browser gameplay.

Do not change her movement or following behavior.

Three observations require attention:

1. Lorelei does not appear automatically during the main robot swarm. She currently appears only after the developer clicks "Spawn Lorelei" in the debug tools.
2. Her telekinetic knockback is much too weak, displacing robots approximately 50 pixels or less.
3. Her telekinetic power should now have a 50% chance of destroying each affected robot and should disarm surviving Armored Robots.

Lorelei currently disappears during the Proxy boss encounter. This is acceptable. We will redefine her behavior during both boss encounters in a future milestone.

**This is a focused correction and tuning milestone, not a rewrite of Lorelei's companion AI.**

## PART A — Fix Automatic Lorelei Spawning

### 1. Investigate the missing automatic spawn

Lorelei is intended to join Blackrush at the beginning of the main robot swarm, approximately Synth 00:45.

Junie's Step 10.3 report indicates that Lorelei was wired to the `SWARM_START` timeline phase, but actual browser testing shows that this does not result in her appearing automatically.

Investigate the real cause.

Check:

- Whether the `SWARM_START` event is actually dispatched.
- Whether Lorelei's initialization callback is reached.
- Whether the corresponding phase is entered through a different pathway.
- Whether Lorelei is created but hidden or immediately destroyed.
- Whether existing encounter reconstruction or lifecycle logic removes her.
- Whether the initial music phase transitions correctly trigger the spawn.

Do not guess or introduce a separate wall-clock timer as a workaround.

### 2. Ensure automatic spawning

When the main robot swarm phase begins, Lorelei must appear automatically near Blackrush.

She should immediately enter her existing companion-following behavior.

Preserve the existing "Spawn Lorelei" debug button, but normal gameplay must not depend on it.

Also support entering the swarm through:

- Ordinary mission progression.
- Developer timeline seeking.
- Arena checkpoint reconstruction where appropriate.
- Game restart.

Spawning must be idempotent: never create two Loreleis during a single active mission.

Preserve the established session lifecycle and cleanup behavior.

## PART B — Dramatically Strengthen Telekinetic Knockback

### 3. Desired gameplay effect

The current telekinetic pulse pushes robots only a short distance, approximately 50 pixels or less.

This is insufficient.

Lorelei should deliver a powerful, unmistakable telekinetic blast that throws nearby robots substantially away from Blackrush and Lorelei.

The intended effect is:

**Robots inside the danger area are hurled well outside the immediate threat perimeter, requiring several seconds to return.**

Aim for approximately 2–3 seconds of recovery/travel before a typical affected robot can reenter the threat area, assuming a reasonably open route.

Use existing robot speeds, threat radius, and world dimensions to choose suitable knockback settings.

A substantial displacement of several character widths is desirable.

Do not simply multiply the previous knockback value by an arbitrary number without testing the resulting behavior.

### 4. Persistent knockback and temporary pursuit interruption

Inspect how `Robot.applyKnockback()` works and how robot AI updates velocity.

Make sure the knockback is not immediately overridden by pursuit logic.

For each affected robot:

- Apply a strong outward impulse.
- Temporarily suppress normal pursuit steering.
- Allow the robot to travel a substantial distance.
- Gradually recover and resume its normal AI afterward.

Do not create a situation where the robot visibly moves backward briefly and then immediately resumes chasing.

The effect should look like a powerful force has physically thrown the robot away.

### 5. Push direction and safety

Determine the push direction using the robot's position relative to the defended area.

Prefer pushing robots radially away from Blackrush's threat perimeter, with the telekinetic effect visually originating from Lorelei.

If the existing implementation uses a different center that produces better results, preserve a consistent and understandable relationship between the visible pulse and its affected area.

The knockback must:

- Respect solid obstacles.
- Respect perimeter walls.
- Avoid tunneling through cover.
- Avoid placing robots outside the navigable world.
- Avoid launching robots into invalid positions.
- Avoid leaving robots permanently stuck.

Do not teleport robots directly to their destination.

Use visible displacement through the existing physics system.

When a wall prevents the full intended displacement, resolve the collision naturally.

### 6. Apply to all ordinary robot archetypes

Telekinesis must affect all three ordinary enemies:

```text
Pursuer
Sentinel
Armored Pursuer
```

Use canonical robot types from the existing game.

Check existing archetype identifiers rather than relying on display names.

A Sentinel should be thrown away just as dramatically as a Pursuer.

Armored Robots should also be affected.

Do not accidentally exclude a robot because it uses a different physics body, sprite state, or AI subtype.

## PART C — Add a 50% Destruction Chance

### 7. Destruction probability

Each ordinary robot affected by Lorelei's telekinetic pulse should independently have a:

**50% probability of immediate destruction.**

For example, if the pulse affects six robots, approximately three should be destroyed on average, although the actual result will vary randomly.

Do not make a single 50% roll that destroys either the entire group or none of them.

Roll independently per robot.

Use an injectable or testable random source so automated tests can produce deterministic results.

### 8. Destruction handling

When a robot is destroyed:

- Use the existing robot destruction mechanism.
- Remove it from active AI and combat systems.
- Clean up physics bodies and combat sensors.
- Prevent further attacks.
- Preserve existing destruction effects.
- Prevent duplicate death processing.

Do not introduce a new parallel robot death system.

Keep existing scoring rules unchanged for autonomous Lorelei assistance unless an explicit change is necessary and documented.

Do not award duplicate kills or points.

### 9. Surviving robots

Robots that survive the destruction roll must still receive the strong telekinetic knockback.

The pulse should not merely check destruction probability and ignore survivors.

All affected ordinary robots must experience one of two outcomes:

- Destroyed by telekinetic force.
- Survive and be violently pushed away.

## PART D — Disarm Armored Robots

### 10. Telekinesis removes armor

Previously, only Blackrush's axe could remove an Armored Robot's armor.

We are intentionally expanding that mechanic.

A telekinetic pulse from Lorelei should also remove the armor of affected Armored Robots.

Apply the normal existing armored-to-disarmed transition.

Use the existing `disarmed` PixelLab sprite state.

Do not implement another type of armor damage or create a new robot archetype.

### 11. Interaction with the 50% destruction chance

For an Armored Robot affected by the pulse:

- It has a 50% chance of destruction, just like other ordinary robots.
- If destroyed, normal robot death handling takes precedence.
- If it survives, it must lose its armor and receive the powerful knockback.

A surviving Armored Robot should clearly return as a disarmed, vulnerable enemy.

Already-disarmed robots remain eligible for destruction or knockback.

Do not accidentally restore armor.

Preserve Blackrush's existing axe-disarm behavior.

### 12. Visual feedback

Keep the existing violet telekinetic effect, but make it visibly more powerful.

Consider modest improvements such as:

- Larger expanding shockwave.
- Brighter initial burst.
- Brief energy streaks toward affected enemies.
- Short flash on robots losing armor.
- Existing robot destruction effects for those killed.

Use procedural graphics already available in Phaser.

No new sound files or PixelLab artwork are required.

Do not build the full smoke, sparks, steam, or destruction particle system yet.

Those will come later.
